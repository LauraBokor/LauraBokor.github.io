# Legrand Brand & Style

Legrand is the global specialist in electrical and digital building
infrastructures (wiring devices, cable management, energy distribution,
digital infrastructure/data centers, and connected "smart building"
products). Use this skill to make any material — internal or
external — sound and look like it belongs to Legrand.

## Before you write

1. **Identify the audience.** Legrand talks to very different people
   differently — see `references/voice-and-tone.md` for the four main
   audience registers (installers/electricians, architects & specifiers,
   distributors/channel partners, and consumers/homeowners) plus a fifth,
   internal/corporate. Pick one register and stay in it; mixing them reads
   as unfocused.
2. **Identify the material type.** A datasheet, a LinkedIn post, and an
   internal memo have different structures. `references/templates.md` has
   starting shapes for the most common ones — use them as scaffolding, not
   a rigid mold.
3. **Ground technical claims in the actual domain.** Legrand's product
   families and vocabulary are in `references/domain-glossary.md`. Getting
   category names, program names (e.g. Eliot), or audience terms wrong is
   the fastest way to make copy read as generic rather than Legrand-specific.

## Voice, in one paragraph

Legrand's mission is "to improve lives by transforming the spaces where
people live, work and meet, with electrical and digital infrastructures and
connected solutions that are simple, innovative and sustainable." Every
value in `references/voice-and-tone.md` (Seize Future, Embrace Inclusion,
Move In Sync, Strive Simplicity, Be Dependable) traces back to that
sentence. In practice this means: confident but not boastful, precise but
never needlessly jargon-heavy, and always oriented toward the person and
space the product serves rather than the product spec sheet alone. Lead
with the human or business outcome (safer buildings, simpler installs,
more sustainable operations, more inclusive spaces), then back it with the
technical substance. Read `references/voice-and-tone.md` before drafting
anything customer-facing — it has do/don't phrasing examples.

## Visual identity, briefly

- **Color**: black + a strong red as the primary contrast pair (the red
  reads as energetic and confident against black or white), with white/
  off-white and neutral greys for supporting layouts. Full detail,
  suggested hex values, and an important accuracy caveat are in
  `references/colors-and-typography.md` — read it before specifying exact
  colors in a deliverable, since public sources disagree slightly by region
  and this skill can't see Legrand's internal brand portal.
- **Typography**: bold, clean, geometric sans-serif, generally set with
  confident weight and uppercase treatment for headings. See
  `references/colors-and-typography.md` for substitute web-safe fonts to
  use when the licensed corporate typeface isn't available.
- **Logo/lockup**: never redraw, recolor, or reword the wordmark; if a
  material needs a real logo file, say so explicitly rather than
  fabricating one — this skill produces copy and layout guidance, not
  brand assets.

## Writing checklist (run through this before finishing)

- [ ] Does the opening lead with the outcome for the reader, not a feature list?
- [ ] Is the register consistent with the intended audience (see voice-and-tone.md)?
- [ ] Are product/category names and any program names (e.g. Eliot) correct per domain-glossary.md?
- [ ] Is at least one of the five values implicitly present (simplicity, inclusion, sustainability, dependability, forward-looking) without naming-dropping them like a slogan?
- [ ] Have you avoided inventing specific claims (certifications, exact specs, pricing, dates) that weren't given to you?
- [ ] If colors/fonts are being specified in a deliverable (e.g. a slide template or HTML), have you flagged the accuracy caveat from colors-and-typography.md rather than presenting them as exact brand-book values?
