# Starting Structures

These are scaffolding, not fill-in-the-blank forms — adapt length and tone
to the actual audience register from `voice-and-tone.md`.

## Email (partner/customer-facing)

1. **Subject line** — concrete outcome or news, not a teaser ("New
   junction-box line cuts install time" beats "Big news from Legrand!").
2. **Opening line** — the one sentence a busy reader needs even if they
   read nothing else.
3. **Body** — 2-3 short paragraphs or a short bullet list: what it is, why
   it matters to *this* reader specifically, what to do next.
4. **Call to action** — one clear next step (a link, a contact, a date).
5. Sign-off matching the register (warmer for consumers, more direct/
   professional for trade and specifier audiences).

## LinkedIn / social post

- Hook line stands alone in a feed — lead with the outcome or a concrete
  detail, not "We're excited to announce."
- 2-4 short lines/paragraphs, plenty of white space, no dense jargon block.
- End with a light call to action or a question if appropriate (varies by
  platform norms) — don't force it if the content doesn't warrant one.
- Keep any product/program naming exact (see domain-glossary.md).

## Product one-pager / datasheet

1. **Header** — product/family name, one-line positioning statement (outcome-first).
2. **At-a-glance benefits** — 3-4 short bullets, benefit-first, spec second in each.
3. **Technical specifications** — precise, in a scannable table or list; no marketing language here, just facts. Never invent a spec value — leave a placeholder like `[spec: TBD]` if not provided.
4. **Compliance/certifications** — only list what's actually been given to you.
5. **Where it fits** — a short note on the use case/space it serves, tying back to the human/building outcome.

## Internal memo / update

- Direct subject line naming the topic and decision/ask, if any.
- Context in 1-2 sentences — assume the reader is busy, not uninformed.
- Body organized by what changed, why, and what's needed from the reader.
- Close with a clear next step or "no action needed."
- Register: more formal than consumer copy, but still plain-spoken — avoid corporate filler phrases ("synergize," "leverage," "circle back").

## Press-style announcement

1. **Headline** — the news, stated plainly (not a pun, not a question).
2. **Lede** — what happened, who it affects, why now, in one paragraph.
3. **Supporting detail** — 2-3 paragraphs with specifics (again: never fabricate quotes, figures, or dates — placeholder them if not supplied).
4. **Boilerplate-style close** — a short paragraph grounding the news in Legrand's mission/domain (electrical and digital building infrastructures) without repeating the whole mission statement verbatim every time — vary the phrasing.
