# Voice & Tone

## The throughline

Mission: "to improve lives by transforming the spaces where people live,
work and meet, with electrical and digital infrastructures and connected
solutions that are simple, innovative and sustainable."

Vision: to be the global reference in electrical and digital building
infrastructures, enabling connected, low-carbon, and inclusive buildings,
and accelerating electrification and energy efficiency.

Values (weave these in as substance, never as a checklist of buzzwords in
the copy itself):
- **Seize Future** — forward-looking, comfortable talking about
  electrification, connectivity, and sustainability trends without
  hype-cycling every product as "revolutionary."
- **Embrace Inclusion** — accessible spaces and accessible products
  (Legrand has a real assistive-technology line); inclusive language for
  people of all abilities and backgrounds.
- **Move In Sync** — collaborative tone; Legrand talks about working
  *with* installers, specifiers, and partners, not just *to* them.
- **Strive Simplicity** — the single most reliable tell of Legrand voice.
  Complex electrical/digital systems explained in plain, confident
  language. Prefer short sentences and concrete nouns over abstract
  corporate-speak.
- **Be Dependable** — safety, reliability, and precision matter more than
  cleverness. Never sacrifice technical accuracy for a punchier line.

Overall register: confident, precise, warm-but-professional, and
outcome-first. Legrand writes like a company that trusts its engineering
and wants the reader to feel capable, not sold to.

## Audience registers

Pick one per piece. Don't blend them within a single document.

### 1. Installers & electricians (trade audience)
Practical, respectful of their expertise, efficient. Lead with what makes
the job faster, safer, or fewer callbacks. Use correct trade terminology
without over-explaining it — this reader knows what a junction box is.
Short sentences, active voice, imperative mood is fine ("Mount the plate,
then...").

- **Do**: "Fewer parts, faster terminations, one tool for the whole job."
- **Don't**: "Our revolutionary paradigm-shifting connector ecosystem
  empowers electricians." (too abstract, too much hype for a technical reader)

### 2. Architects & specifiers
More technical depth, comfortable with standards/compliance language
(codes, certifications, sustainability ratings), and interested in how a
product fits a larger design intent (space planning, aesthetics,
building performance). Slightly more formal than the trade register, but
still plain-spoken — avoid marketing adjectives that don't carry
information.

- **Do**: "Specify once, adapt everywhere: the same wiring device family
  scales from a single office to a full campus rollout."
- **Don't**: "Our best-in-class solution redefines the category." (no
  concrete claim a specifier can act on)

### 3. Distributors & channel partners
Business-outcome focused — margin, shelf/catalog fit, customer demand,
training and support available to them. Treat them as a partner in growing
a market, not just a reseller.

### 4. Consumers & homeowners (smart building / Eliot connected products)
Warmest and most benefit-led register. Talk about comfort, control, peace
of mind, and simplicity of everyday use — not volts and amps. This is the
one register where lifestyle language belongs.

- **Do**: "Dim the lights, lock the door, check who's at the front step —
  all from your phone."
- **Don't**: "IoT-enabled connected object ecosystem leveraging cloud
  infrastructure." (jargon a homeowner doesn't need)

### 5. Internal / corporate (memos, investor-adjacent, CSR)
More formal, values-forward, comfortable citing strategy pillars, ESG/CSR
commitments, and long-term vision. Still avoid empty corporate filler —
Legrand's public reporting tends to be specific and numbers-forward
(targets, percentages, timelines) rather than vague aspiration.

## General principles

- **Lead with outcome, follow with mechanism.** State what changes for the
  reader or the building, then explain how the product does it.
- **Plain language over jargon**, except where the audience's own trade
  vocabulary is more precise (installers, specifiers) — then use their
  words correctly.
- **Concrete over vague.** "Cuts install time by half" beats "faster and
  more efficient." If you don't have the real number, describe the
  mechanism concretely instead of inventing a stat.
- **Confident, not boastful.** Avoid superlatives ("best," "revolutionary,"
  "game-changing") unless backed by a specific, given claim.
- **Human-centered even in technical copy.** Somewhere in the piece,
  connect the technical detail back to a person, a room, or a task —
  Legrand's brand is about spaces people actually live and work in, not
  components in the abstract.
- **Never fabricate specifics.** Don't invent certifications, exact specs,
  pricing, launch dates, or statistics that weren't provided — ask for
  them or leave a clearly marked placeholder instead.
