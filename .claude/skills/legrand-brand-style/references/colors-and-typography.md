# Colors, Typography & Logo Rules

## Accuracy caveat — read this first

Legrand does not publish a single public pixel-perfect brand book, and the
exact Pantone/hex/font-license values live in Legrand's internal brand
portal, which this skill has no access to. The values below are
reconstructed from Legrand's public logo, packaging, and marketing
materials, and are a reliable *starting point for the look and feel* —
correct hue, correct contrast pattern, correct typographic character. They
are **not guaranteed to be the exact certified values** used in official
collateral. If a deliverable needs certified-exact color (print production,
a template that will carry the real logo, anything going to an external
print vendor), say so and tell the user to confirm hex/Pantone/font license
against Legrand's internal brand center before it ships. Don't present the
values below as if they came from that portal.

## Color palette

**Primary pair**
- Black — near-black, used for the wordmark and as the dominant neutral. `#0A0A0A`–`#1A1A1A` range.
- Legrand red — the brand's signature accent, used for the wordmark and as
  the single strongest highlight color. Treat it as a warm, saturated red
  rather than orange-red or pink-red. `#E2001A`–`#D5001C` range is a
  reasonable default; some regional Legrand entities run slightly warmer
  (toward `#D54401`) or slightly brighter (toward `#ED413D`).

**Supporting neutrals**
- White `#FFFFFF` and a soft off-white (`#F5F4F0`–`#FAFAF8`) for backgrounds and breathing room.
- Mid greys (`#6B6B6B`–`#9A9A9A`) for secondary text, captions, and dividers.
- Charcoal (`#333333`–`#3A3A3A`) as an alternative to pure black for large text blocks, easier to read at length.

**Usage pattern**
- Red is an accent, not a background flood — use it for a single CTA, a key stat, a headline word, or a thin structural line. A page that is mostly red reads as off-brand; Legrand's red works because it's used sparingly against black/white/grey.
- Black or charcoal carries most body text and structure. White/off-white is the dominant field color.
- Avoid pairing the red with green or blue as co-equal accents — it competes with the brand's black/red/white restraint. Grey and white are the safe supporting colors.

## Typography

Legrand's identity uses a bold, geometric sans-serif with clean, confident
lines — headlines and the wordmark itself lean toward tight letter-spacing
and, in many applications, uppercase or condensed treatments for emphasis.
The company describes its overall design philosophy as "georganic" —
geometric pragmatism balanced with organic, human-centered sensibility —
which shows up as clean grids and straight lines softened by rounded
terminals and generous white space, rather than a purely mechanical/
industrial look.

Because the specific licensed corporate typeface isn't publicly
documented, use these substitutes for materials produced with this skill:

- **Headings**: a bold, slightly condensed geometric sans — e.g. Inter
  (Bold/Black), Helvetica Neue Bold Condensed, Arial Narrow Bold, or Neue
  Haas Grotesk. Uppercase or title case works well for short headlines.
- **Body text**: a clean, highly legible sans at regular/medium weight —
  e.g. Inter, Helvetica, Arial, or system-ui — prioritizing readability
  over character for long-form copy (datasheets, technical instructions).
- **Avoid**: serif fonts, script/handwriting fonts, and anything overtly
  playful or decorative — these clash with Legrand's engineered,
  professional register.

## Logo & lockup rules

- Never redraw, recolor, distort, or rewrite the "Legrand" wordmark. If a
  deliverable needs the actual logo file, tell the user to source it from
  Legrand's brand center rather than having it recreated here.
- The Legrand emblem (a geometric mark evoking an electrical socket/
  connection) is a fixed graphic asset — don't attempt to describe new
  variations of it as if they were official.
- Maintain clear space around any logo lockup; don't crowd it with body
  text or competing graphic elements.
- When mocking up a layout (e.g. an HTML wireframe or slide template) that
  will eventually carry the real logo, use a clearly-labeled placeholder
  (e.g. a text box reading "LEGRAND LOGO") rather than fabricating a
  lookalike mark.
